Helina is a first year honors International Affairs major at Howard University. A member of the Humanities and Social Sciences Scholars Program (cohort four), her research interests center on international policy and global health outcomes, specifically how access to health care affects adolescents' academic self efficacy, and thus, opportunities. Helina explores this through work in faculty-led research labs at Howard, including the Global Community Health Lab and the Quantitative Histories Lab Workshop. On campus, she is involved in several student organizations, including the National Association for the Advancement of Colored People (Unit 7700-B), the Ethiopian-Eritrean Student Association, Black Professionals in International Affairs, and the Bunche Brigade. Ultimately, Helina aims to accumulate experience in college that will allow her to do research that impact and improve the opportunities and health outcomes of communities globally.

This group project analyzed sales data from the data set "Bean There Coffee Co.," covering transactions across three locations (Campus, Downtown, and Airport) from March to August 2025. After cleaning the data by checking for missing values, duplicates, and zero or negative prices, and creating new columns such as revenue and hour of day, we created a bar chart, countplot, scatter plot, and correlation heatmap to answer practical business questions about what sells, where, and for how much. The analysis found that coffee drives about 62% of sales at every store, with lattes as the top seller. Campus and Downtown lead in transaction volume, while the Airport earns more per sale because its items are priced higher. Sales peak in the morning, around 10 to 11am. After our visualization and analysis, we suggest that an effective next step would be studying how discounts affect customer spending.
